01

Research interests

Every question below came out of shipping AI to real users. I'd like to study them properly, with frontier models, controlled experiments and results others can build on.

AI control

Oversight gates that stay meaningful

I run a multi-agent LLM system where consequential actions go through risk checks and human approval gates. When does a gate actually catch a bad action, and when does approval turn into a formality? How should control protocols for agents be designed so oversight holds up as agents get more capable?

Scalable oversight

Human edits as an oversight signal

The most useful data I've found for improving AI-drafted text is what its human reviewers delete and rewrite. How far can cheap, implicit signals like edit traces scale as a measure of where a model is ungrounded, and where do they mislead?

Safeguards

Failing closed under pressure

A customer-facing guardrail is only useful if, when it breaks, the system says nothing rather than something confidently wrong. I want to measure how robust classifiers and kill-switches are under adversarial and out-of-distribution inputs, and how to make their failure modes predictable.

Evaluation

Evals that predict deployed behaviour

At Amazon AGI I built scoring pipelines across Nova model iterations; at Tesla I see how deployed models behave with real customers. I'm interested in the gap between offline eval scores and behaviour in deployment, and in evals that close it.

Tesla · 2026 · Evaluation from human feedback

Grounding AI-generated repair notes

Problem
When AI drafts technicians' repair notes, every edit costs technician time, and a note that doesn't match the repair shouldn't reach a customer.
Approach
Treated technicians' edits as labelled data. I analysed what they consistently added and removed, traced each pattern to a cause, and made targeted changes rather than broad prompt rewrites.
Result
Edit and deletion rates fell substantially in every region, saving technician time and keeping notes grounded in the actual repair.

Tesla · 2026 · Safeguards

Guardrailed AI replies to customers

Problem
Letting AI answer more customer messages without letting wrong or harmful replies through.
Approach
Worked with developers and stakeholders to put guardrails and fail-closed switches around customer-facing outputs, then found new message types where the AI could reply safely and decisions that should go to a human advisor.
Result
Significantly more messages answered automatically, with faster first responses, and AI replies still trusted.

Tesla · 2026 · AI control in practice

Agent systems with human approval gates

System
Designed and run a multi-agent LLM system for monitoring, reporting and drafting. Agent actions go through risk checks and human approval gates before they take effect.
Also
Lead the use of AI agents to simulate service workflows, and work on making voice AI more autonomous and consistent with written channels.
Why it matters
This is AI control in production: deciding which actions an agent may take alone, which need a human, and how to tell the difference.

Amazon AGI · 2025–26 · LLM evaluation

Evaluation pipelines for Amazon Nova

Built
Prompt evaluation and scoring frameworks that measure LLM output quality, consistency and safety at scale. Python pipelines for Amazon Nova and Bedrock supported large-scale scoring across model iterations.
Found
RAG workflows that assess retrieval accuracy and generation quality, surfacing failure modes to model teams. Reliability checks for data-quality issues, anomalies and edge cases that affected model performance.
Also
Prepared fine-tuning data and evaluated fine-tuned outputs. Ran everything in containerised Docker/Linux environments for reproducibility, and documented pipeline architecture, data schemas and AI quality risks for responsible-AI governance.
